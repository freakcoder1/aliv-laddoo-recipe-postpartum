## Body

### What Is Aliv Laddoo?
Aliv laddoo is a traditional postpartum sweet from Maharashtra, Gujarat, and parts of North India. It is made by soaking aliv (halim or garden cress) seeds, then mixing them with whole wheat flour, jaggery, and ghee to form small round balls.

### Why New Mothers Eat It
After childbirth, women need:
- Iron to rebuild red blood cells lost during delivery
- Energy for healing and breastfeeding
- Easy-to-digest nutrition during recovery

Aliv seeds are a plant source of iron, and the ghee and jaggery in the laddoo provide energy. A 2003 study in Nutrition and Health analyzed traditional Indian lactation foods and found that aliv (ajwain in that study) had the highest protein, fiber, and mineral content among all tested foods.

### Traditional Recipe
**Ingredients:**
- Aliv (halim) seeds: 2 tablespoons
- Whole wheat flour: 1 cup
- Jaggery: 1/2 cup (adjust to taste)
- Ghee: 1/4 cup
- Nuts (almonds, cashews): a small handful
- Cardamom powder: 1/2 teaspoon
- Milk: as needed

**Method:**
1. Soak aliv seeds in water for 2-3 hours until they swell into a gel
2. Dry roast the whole wheat flour in a pan until fragrant
3. In the same pan, heat ghee and sautÃ© the soaked aliv seeds briefly
4. Add the roasted wheat flour and mix well
5. Add jaggery, cardamom powder, and chopped nuts
6. Add milk gradually until the mixture holds together
7. Shape into small laddoos while warm

### How Much to Eat
One small laddoo once or twice a day is a common amount during the postpartum period. It is a food, not a medicine.

### Safety Notes
Aliv is traditionally avoided during pregnancy because it is believed to stimulate the uterus. There is little research either way, so it is sensible to avoid it and ask your doctor.
